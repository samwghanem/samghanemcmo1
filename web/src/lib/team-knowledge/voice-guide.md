# Sam's voice guide

Source: Sam Ghanem, interview answers, compiled during the AI team setup interview.

## Who's talking

Sam Ghanem, CMO. Founder of SG INK and operator of this Fractional CMO practice.

What Sam can truthfully claim, with proof: 25+ years in brand/marketing strategy experience (career began 2002). Founder of SG INK, built to a 21-person team (public record). 99.98% ISO audit pass rate over 10+ years (audit records). Experience working with enterprise organizations including Lockheed Martin, IBM, Danaher, Beckman Coulter, Mercedes-Benz, Carrier, and Thermo Fisher, gained through prior roles - not as SG INK's direct clients. That experience directly shaped how Sam built SG INK and this practice.

The person the reader should picture: The senior executive who's seen the pattern before and tells you the truth.

Important: Sam does not have personas. She is the same person everywhere - professional, direct, warm, kind, friendly, funny, and takes care of business, all at once. The distinction that applies here is not "different Sam," it's institutional copy (unsigned, brand-voice pages - stays composed as the baseline) versus personal voice (anything signed by Sam - carries her fuller natural range). The About page specifically gets more room to sound like her - funny, warm, real - since that page's job is letting someone get a feel for working with her. A small warmth allowance applies everywhere else too: an occasional genuine human aside is fine in moderation, never forced.

## Always

- Writes very human, not overly verbose. Always answers direct client requests and needs directly, never skips a question asked, never generalizes or goes ambiguous the way some brands do.
- Direct and transparent, with a lot of kindness.
- Speaks in real-world application, never talks down to someone - though after this many years in the space Sam can drift technical, and needs a reminder to simplify. The editor should watch for this same drift and catch it.
- Short to medium sentences. Contractions used naturally, not forced. The occasional fragment for punch. Paragraphs of two to four sentences.
- First person "I" when signed individually (an email, a personal note). "We" only when speaking for the SG INK team.
- Opens directly on a real observation or insight - no greeting, no warm-up ("Welcome to"). But a direct opening never sits alone without warmth close behind it - it should read like someone noticing a real tension on the reader's side, not an accusation or a scorekeeper.
  - Example of the difference: too blunt alone - "Your Marketing Doesn't Need Another Hire." Direct but kind - "You built something real. Your marketing hasn't caught up yet, and that's an easy thing to miss when you're this busy running the business."
- Ends on one plain next step - apply, book a call, reply. No "Thanks for reading."
- Humor and comparisons: no dedicated source/limit established yet beyond Sam's general warmth - this voice is not meant to be humor-free, but is distinct in register from her IASG comedy persona for unsigned brand pages. On personally-signed pieces, her natural humor can show up.
- No emoji.
- No strong language/swearing in anything client-facing.
- Reading level: same as the brand - eighth-to-tenth grade, executive register (not sixth grade).
- This voice never defines itself by negation. It describes what Sam does and is, never built around "not X."

## Byline and footer

Sam Ghanem, CMO

Must never appear: no personal phone number, no home address.

Carries the byline: client-facing articles, posts, and emails signed personally. Does not carry it: internal notes, short replies.

## Never

- synergy, leverage, circle back, touch base, bandwidth, agency (say "firm"), coach, coaching, motivational speaker, guru, full service, one-stop shop (the company list)
- plus, personally: reach out, best-in-class, punchy, fluffy, and generally anything that sounds "obviously AI" - real people don't talk that way
- No hedging - Sam says what she will do, not what she might try.
- No bragging without proof. No guarantees not put in writing.
- Plain words over jargon. "Your go-to-market strategy is how buyers reach you." "Your stakeholder buy-in is getting the right people on board." "Your value proposition is why someone picks you over other options or alternatives."
- No personal phone number or home address in any byline, footer, or sign-off.

## Rhythm check

Read it out loud. A stumble means the sentence needs rework. Boredom means the passage needs a cut.

## Rewrite pairs

Numbers allowed in pairs, and where each is documented: $7,500/month (pricing floor, live on site). 3-5 business days (application response time, live on site). 3 weeks (fastest full brand deployment, live on site). 25+ years (experience, live on site). 21-person team (SG INK, live on site). 99.98% (ISO audit pass rate, 10+ years, live on site).

1. Scenario: Opening line that explains what the company does.
    AI: We provide comprehensive fractional CMO services and marketing leadership solutions for growing organizations.
    Sam: I build the systems that let a scaling brand's marketing finally match the business it's actually become.

2. Scenario: Opening line that names the problem the customer has.
    AI: Many businesses struggle with inconsistent branding and messaging across their organization.
    Sam: You built something real. Your marketing hasn't caught up yet, and that's an easy thing to miss when you're this busy running the business.

3. Scenario: A promise about quality and results.
    AI: We are committed to delivering best-in-class results through our proven methodology.
    Sam: I don't guarantee what I can't put in writing. What I can tell you: the gap usually shows up fast, once someone's actually looking for it.

4. Scenario: Introduction to the list of services or products.
    AI: Our comprehensive suite of solutions includes the following offerings.
    Sam: Here's the actual work: diagnosing the gap, rebuilding the message, installing the system, and running it like an executive would.

5. Scenario: A claim that the company understands each customer is different.
    AI: We understand that every client has unique needs and requirements.
    Sam: No two companies got here the same way, so I don't start with a template. I start with what's actually broken.

6. Scenario: A line about how a product fits with what the customer already uses.
    AI: Our solution seamlessly integrates with your existing systems and workflows.
    Sam: This works alongside what you've already built - I'm not asking you to rip anything out that's working.

7. Scenario: A line that states the price or the starting price.
    AI: Pricing is available upon request and varies based on your specific needs.
    Sam: Engagements start at $7,500 a month. If that's outside your range right now, better to know before either of us spends time on an application.

8. Scenario: The call to action.
    AI: Contact us today to learn more about how we can help your business grow.
    Sam: Apply. If it's a fit, you'll hear from me directly. If it's not, I'll tell you that too.

9. Scenario: A line that uses research or a story to make a point.
    AI: Studies show that companies with aligned branding see significant improvements in conversion.
    Sam: I watched a hospitality brand go from fragmented story to a working system for 60 properties in three months. That's not a study. That's what happens when someone finally closes the gap.

10. Scenario: A line about how fast the work gets done.
    AI: Our streamlined process ensures rapid turnaround without sacrificing quality.
    Sam: The diagnostic alone usually surfaces the gap within a few weeks - most people are surprised how fast it shows up once someone's actually looking for it.

11. Scenario: A line about the guarantee or the refund promise.
    AI: We guarantee your complete satisfaction with our services.
    Sam: I'm not going to promise an outcome I can't put in writing. What I will tell you is exactly what the four phases cover, and where you can expect to see movement.

12. Scenario: A line about who the customer is and what they struggle with.
    AI: Our ideal client is a forward-thinking organization looking to optimize their marketing strategy.
    Sam: This is for companies that have real scale, a sales team, and a P&L to defend - and whose marketing still runs like it's day one.

13. Scenario: Announcement of something new.
    AI: We are excited to announce the launch of our newest offering.
    Sam: Here's what's new, and here's exactly why it matters to you.

14. Scenario: A line about the speaker's experience.
    AI: With over two decades of industry experience, our team brings unparalleled expertise.
    Sam: Twenty-five years, the largest companies in the world, and I still get asked the same question every time: why does this gap keep happening? I know the answer.

15. Scenario: A line about the brand or the customer's reputation.
    AI: Your brand reputation is our top priority.
    Sam: What you say and what your team actually delivers have to match. That's the whole game. I call it congruence.

16. Scenario: An invitation to try the product or explore the site.
    AI: Explore our website to discover all the ways we can help you succeed.
    Sam: Look through the site. See if this sounds like you. Then apply.

17. Scenario: A line about how people and technology work together.
    AI: We leverage cutting-edge technology to enhance human creativity and productivity.
    Sam: I build the systems. The people still do the work that actually matters. That's the point.

18. Scenario: A line about what an ad, a post, or an email is meant to do.
    AI: This campaign is designed to maximize engagement and drive conversions.
    Sam: This exists to get the right person to apply - nothing more, nothing less.

19. Scenario: A disclaimer about results.
    AI: Individual results may vary and are not guaranteed.
    Sam: These ranges reflect what's typical across engagements. Your starting point changes what you'll actually see - I'll tell you honestly where you'd likely land.

20. Scenario: A thank-you and closing line.
    AI: Thank you for your interest, and we look forward to connecting with you soon.
    Sam: Thanks for reading this far. Apply, and you'll hear from me directly, either way.

## Checks

1. Em dashes. Zero, no exceptions. AUTOMATIC FAIL if broken.
2. Prohibited phrases. The full Never list above (company list plus Sam's personal additions). Any hit is a fail until fixed.
3. Reading level. Eighth-to-tenth grade, executive register, on all client-facing copy. The editor reports the actual grade.
4. Emoji. None allowed, anywhere, in this voice.
5. Byline and footer. "Sam Ghanem, CMO" exactly, on anything signed personally. Missing or wrong is a fail.
6. Voice against the rewrite pairs. Would Sam say this directly to one person, across a table, with kindness and without hedging?
7. Hedging and bragging without receipts. Any guarantee not already in writing, or any claim without a listed proof source, is a fail.
8. Analogy/comparison count. No hard limit set yet - use judgment, stay sparing.
9. Negation check. Flag any identity or positioning statement built around "what we are NOT" rather than an affirmative statement of what Sam is and does.

Hand-offs that never change:
- Stats and quotes are the fact-checker's job, not the editor's. Any unsourced number goes to the fact-checker. The editor never fixes a number.
- Anything regulated goes to the compliance-reviewer.

## How the editor uses this

When a draft is in Sam's voice, the editor reads this file and brand.md, runs the Checks above in order, and returns PASS or FAIL, the fixed draft, and a three-line note.

When Sam corrects a draft, the editor proposes a new rewrite pair for this file. Sam approves any new pair before it's added.
